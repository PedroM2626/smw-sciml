### 10.69 The Simple Reward, and a Stage No Agent Had Cleared

10.66 and 10.68 left two of the four sessions the map hands over unconcluded: `0x29` and `0x27` ended in the death mode under every agent this repository had, and 10.68 read that as the sessions themselves - a scripted ride that kills a player who never touches a button, and a floor of decor over air. Every one of those agents was shaped by an objective with many terms (progress, velocity, an alive bonus, pit and hazard penalties, a leap bonus). A sibling project on the Nintendo DS cleared its stages with the opposite choice, and this section asks the same question here: what does the simplest reward do on the stages nothing concluded?

**The reward.** Pay only for a new maximum of horizontal progress, in tiles, and add a fixed bonus when the engine itself says the stage was cleared:

$$r_t = \frac{\max(0,\; x_t - \max_{s \lt t} x_s)}{16} \;+\; 10 \cdot \mathbb 1[\text{clear}]$$

The episode ends on a death (the mode byte passes `0x0B` or a life is lost), when the session leaves `0x14`, or after 600 frames with no new maximum. Nothing rewards speed and nothing punishes a detour, so backing up for a run-up, waiting for an enemy to pass or standing still costs nothing - which is exactly what the shaped objectives priced against. A clear is the 10.66 verdict, read off the engine: the run leaves `0x14` without passing through `0x0B` and without losing a life; once the end-level timer at `$7E:1493` is set the controls are released and the engine is given the frames to finish its own exit sequence.

**The agent.** `src/training/potential_reward_ga.py` searches the 2344 weights of a one-hidden-layer MLP with an elitist genetic algorithm (population 48, 6 elites, Gaussian mutation 0.15, seed 0). The observation is 64 numbers read straight off the engine every 4 frames: horizontal and vertical velocity and the ground flag, the measured tile classes of 10.68 in a 7x7 window from one column behind Mario to five ahead, and the three nearest engine-running sprites. The policy picks one of 8 button combinations and holds it for the 4 frames, B included: jump height in this engine is how long B is held, so a policy that released it mid-hold could only ever hop. The fitness of a genome is the return of one deterministic episode from the stage's savestate, so no value function is needed; the 10.60 A pulse answers the stage's message boxes.

| stage | savestate | best progress | episode | generations | the engine's verdict | best agent before (10.66) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `0x29` | `smw_map_node_68_135_t29.state` | 4808.0625 px | 3288 frames to the goal tape | cleared in generation 57 | **cleared** (left `0x14` for `0x0C`, no `0x0B`, no life lost) | `dagger`, 406.4375 px, died |
| `0x27` | `smw_map_round_1_152_104_t27.state` | 528.0625 px | 400 frames | 150, no clear | no (death mode, a life taken) | `dagger`, 372.8750 px, died |

**`0x29` is cleared.** The first stage in this repository concluded by a learned agent other than the published clearance controller, from the same savestate 10.66 recorded its dashboard from. Replayed in a fresh emulator the genome reproduces the run to the frame, and the engine's own trace is the clear signature of 10.66: the goal tape sets the end-level timer, the mode goes `0x14` to `0x0C` to `0x0D` to `0x0E`, and the map comes back with the lives counter untouched. The run reproduced to the same generation, distance and frame count when the study was re-run into `results/`. What it is *not* is a robust policy: the fitness is one deterministic episode, so the genome is evidence that the stage can be concluded under this reward, not a clear rate.

**`0x27` is not, and the reason is now visible.** The best genome runs, takes the longest jump the engine allows off the last ledge, and falls short of a tall pillar beyond the gap. The way across in the stage is a platform on a chain that the player has to wait for and ride, which a search that only ever restarts from the stage's first frame does not find in 150 generations: the progress curve is flat for the last hundred. That is an exploration limit, not a reward one - the reward already pays for reaching the pillar however long the wait - and the next step it points at is restarting episodes from savestates along the frontier rather than a different objective.

**What this corrects in 10.68.** The `0x29` session does not kill "a player who never touches a button" by its own machinery: a red Koopa walks into a small Mario standing at the entry, and an agent that moves clears the stage. The `0x27` "decor over air" is the row of dotted outline blocks the engine draws for switch-palace blocks that have not been activated - intangible, so the measured tile table is right to call them decor - and the stage is crossed above them, not on them. Both sessions are playable stages; 10.68's tables stand as measurements of the agents it flew, and Section 12 records the reading that is withdrawn.

Regenerate: `make potential-ga` (or `smw-pinn potential-ga`; needs core + ROM), and `python -m src.training.potential_reward_ga --state smw_map_round_1_152_104_t27.state` for `0x27`. Artifacts: `results/potential_ga_smw_map_node_68_135_t29_metrics.json`, `results/potential_ga_smw_map_round_1_152_104_t27_metrics.json`; genomes under `results/checkpoints/potential_ga_*.npz`.
